Bio

My research lies at the intersection of statistics, machine learning, and computational biology. I develop statistical and computational methods for high-dimensional biomedical data, with a particular focus on RNA epigenetics and epitranscriptomics, transcriptomics, single-cell and spatial omics, and multi-omics integration.

I am especially interested in methodological problems motivated by modern sequencing technologies, including inference for RNA modifications, differential analysis, representation learning, and robust statistical modeling of complex biological data. More broadly, my research aims to develop interpretable and principled computational methods that connect large-scale molecular measurements with underlying biological mechanisms.

I am currently an Assistant Professor in the School of Data Science at The Chinese University of Hong Kong, Shenzhen. I received my Ph.D. in Biostatistics from Emory University in 2022. Before that, I received my M.S. in Operational Research from the Academy of Mathematics and Systems Science, Chinese Academy of Sciences in 2017, and my B.S. in Applied Mathematics from Hunan University in 2014.
